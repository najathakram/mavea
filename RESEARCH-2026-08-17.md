# Pre-launch research: payments, WooCommerce, Hostinger, domain (2026-08-17)

Verified against live sources on 2026-08-17, ahead of moving MAVÉA onto Hostinger and
configuring `mavea.lk`. **Section 1 lists the things this research contradicts in the existing
plan docs** — read that first, because two of them move real money.

Where a figure could not be read at the vendor's own page it is marked
**[unverified-at-primary]**: `payhere.lk/fees` and `support.payhere.lk` both sit behind a
Cloudflare bot challenge that blocks automated fetching, so those numbers are triangulated
across independent secondary sources that agree with each other.

## 2. Sri Lankan payments: the real landscape

### 2.1 The single most useful fact

**HelaPay is not a gateway you integrate.** It is Sri Lanka's biometric wallet, built by
Helakuru and *powered by PayHere*. Every PayHere merchant is already enabled to accept it —
there is no separate application, plugin, or contract. The repo's 2026-08-10 note on this was
right and remains right.

It also carries **the cheapest rate on the board: 1.99% on every PayHere tier**, versus
2.69–3.30% for cards. That is a 1.3-point saving on the same sale. This is a pricing lever the
plan has not used: *surfacing HelaPay ahead of cards at checkout is worth roughly Rs. 160 on a
Rs. 12,000 order*, at zero integration cost.

### 2.2 Gateway comparison

| Gateway | Txn fee | Setup | Monthly | Settlement | Woo plugin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PayHere Lite** | 3.30% cards / **1.99% HelaPay** | Free | Free | 2–3 days | ✅ official, 2,000+ installs |
| **PayHere Plus** | 2.99% / 1.99% | Free | Rs. 3,990 | 2–3 days | same |
| **PayHere Premium** | 2.69% / 1.99% | Free | higher | 2–3 days | same |
| WebXPay | ~2.5–3.5% | ~Rs. 15,000 | ~Rs. 4,990+ | next-day | partial |
| Genie (Dialog) | ~1.5%+ | Free | Free | 1–3 days | not confirmed |
| OnePay | 3.5% cards / **1% LankaQR** | ~Rs. 1,500 | varies | 1–3 days | not confirmed |
| Commercial Bank SimplePay | 2.5–3% | ~Rs. 15,000 | ~Rs. 36,000/yr | 2–5 days | ✗ |
| Sampath | 3–5% | ~Rs. 15,000 | ~Rs. 75,000/yr | 2–5 days | ✗ |
| HNB | ~4% | Rs. 15–25,000 | ~Rs. 60,000+/yr | 2–5 days | ✗ |

**[unverified-at-primary]** for the PayHere rows.

**Ruling: PayHere Lite stays the right launch choice**, unchanged. Bank gateways want
Rs. 15,000 up front plus five-figure annual fees before the first sale — indefensible at 5–30
orders/day. Genie's headline ~1.5% is tempting but has no confirmed WooCommerce plugin, which
would mean custom integration work for a second gateway the store does not need.

**When to move to Plus:** Rs. 3,990/mo buys 0.31 points (3.30→2.99). Break-even is
**Rs. 1.29M/month in card volume**. Below that, Lite is cheaper — so launch on Lite and revisit
only when monthly card revenue clears ~Rs. 1.3M. Note Lite also caps at Rs. 50,000/payment and
**Rs. 200,000/month**, which a good month would breach; that ceiling, not the rate, is what will
force the upgrade first.

### 2.3 BNPL — Koko and Mintpay

Koko's own support desk states **12% commission on total order value**, varying by merchant
agreement. For context, BNPL in other markets runs 2–8%. Against a brand positioned as "the
affordable sister," 12% is more than four times the card rate and roughly a fifth of a typical
apparel gross margin.

The plugin is also weak: `paykoko-bnpl-payment-gateway` v3.0.4, **30+ active installs, zero
reviews**. It does support WooCommerce Blocks and has sandbox mode, but 30 installs means
almost no field hardening.

**Recommendation: keep BNPL out of launch**, as the plan already sequences it. If it goes in
later, gate it to carts ≥ Rs. 10,000 and price the 12% in, or it eats the margin the whole
positioning depends on. Mintpay remains the healthier-ecosystem fallback worth applying to in
parallel — it has a DFCC Bank partnership — but its merchant rate is not published and must be
asked for directly.

## 3. Installing the gateways

### 3.1 PayHere (launch — day 3–7)

Plugin `payhere-payment-gateway` v2.4.5 · 2,000+ installs · WP 6.4+ · tested to 6.9.7 ·
**Blocks supported since 2.3.0**. HPOS compatibility is *not declared* — test it, and if the
declaration is missing WooCommerce will warn; keep HPOS in compatibility/sync mode for the
first weeks as §3 of the plan already says.

1. Site must already be **live on the final domain with Privacy, Refund, Terms, Delivery and
   Contact pages published** — PayHere checks this at approval.
2. Apply at payhere.lk. Domain must match **exactly**, including www vs non-www.
3. Install plugin → WooCommerce → Settings → Payments → PayHere → Manage.
4. Enter Merchant ID + Merchant Secret. Tick "Enable On-site checkout" if wanted.
5. Sandbox mode on → test with the sandbox cards in the merchant dashboard → confirm the order
   moves to `processing`.
6. Sandbox off → **live Rs. 100 transaction → then refund it** before opening the store.

Two traps specific to this build, already flagged in the risk register and still live:
- **Amount hashing.** PayHere hashes a 2-decimal amount while the store displays 0 decimals in
  LKR. Sandbox-test odd totals (e.g. Rs. 12,345) or hashes will mismatch.
- **`notify_url` must be publicly reachable.** It cannot be tested from `local/` Docker without
  a tunnel. This is why the plan sequences PayHere after go-live.

Tokenized/automatic payments need App ID + App Secret and are **Premium-plan only** — not
needed here.

### 3.2 Koko (phase 2, if ever)

Plugin `paykoko-bnpl-payment-gateway` v3.0.4 · WP 5.8+ · PHP 7.4+ · WooCommerce 5.0+ · Blocks
supported.

1. Merchant account first: `merchant.support@paykoko.com` or 011 765 7167. Ask for the
   commission in writing, the WooCommerce onboarding path, and the timeline.
2. Install → WooCommerce → Settings → Payments → enable "Koko: Buy Now Pay Later".
3. Enter **Merchant ID, API Key, Public Key, Private Key** (an RSA key pair — Koko issues it).
4. Sandbox mode uses `qaapi.paykoko.com`; live uses `prodapi.paykoko.com`. Toggle off to go live.
5. It renders instalment breakdowns on product and cart pages — check those against the
   no-discount-theatre house rule before enabling, since "3 × Rs. 4,000" reads as price framing.

Callbacks are verified by OpenSSL signature. The v3.0.4 changelog specifically fixed server
callback handling for JSON vs form-encoded payloads, which suggests callbacks were unreliable
before — worth a hard test if adopted.

### 3.3 What needs no integration at all

COD, bank transfer (BACS + WhatsApp slip), and **HelaPay/eZ Cash/mCash/Genie/LankaQR/Sampath
Vishwa via the single PayHere account**. One application covers the entire local wallet
landscape. Nothing else should be integrated at launch.

## 4. WooCommerce state of the art, August 2026

**Current: 11.0.1** (2026-08-10), following 11.0 (2026-08-04, 551 PRs). Both 10.9 and 11.0
require **database updates** — take a backup before either, and run them on staging first.

What landed recently that matters here:
- **11.0**: guest shoppers can **claim previous guest orders** into an account — directly useful
  for a COD-heavy store where most first orders are guest. The **block email editor** arrived,
  and the block *product* editor was **removed entirely**. HPOS got faster.
- **10.9**: checkout and dashboard performance, plus **transactional email logging** — genuinely
  valuable for diagnosing the email-optional COD flow.
- **Abandoned cart/checkout recovery** is now a first-party feature.

### The checkout blocks question — the plan's reasoning needs updating

The plan forbids block checkout partly because "PayHere has no block integration." **That is no
longer true** (PayHere 2.3.0+, and Koko too). The decision should not rest on it.

The honest current position:
- Blocks have been the **default for new installs since 8.3**, and WooCommerce states most
  development effort now goes to blocks. Classic is on a slow path to legacy.
- Blocks *do* now support custom fields via `woocommerce_register_additional_checkout_field`
  (register on `woocommerce_init`), with `woocommerce_validate_additional_field` and
  `woocommerce_sanitize_additional_field` hooks, and locations `contact` / `address` / `order`.
- **But the API supports only three field types: text, select, checkbox.** And `woocommerce_checkout_fields` — the filter this build's entire checkout plugin is written against — does nothing in blocks.

For MAVÉA specifically, blocks would struggle with: hiding `address_2`, making postcode
optional, renaming order notes, phone regex normalisation to `+947…`, **conditional
email-required-only-when-PayHere-selected**, and COD fee/gating by cart value and RTO history.
Several are possible; the conditional-required logic and field removal are the awkward ones.

**Ruling: stay on classic shortcode checkout for launch** — the conclusion in the plan survives,
and the forbidden-action note stays. But record the real reason (field-logic depth, not gateway
support), and treat classic as **technical debt with a horizon**, not a permanent choice. Revisit
after launch when order flow is stable.

### Performance targets

2026 thresholds: **LCP < 2.5s, INP < 200ms, CLS < 0.1**, mobile-first. For this store:
- The PDP hero image is almost always the LCP element. **Do not lazy-load the first 1–2 images** —
  lazy-loading the LCP image actively delays it. This is the single most common WooCommerce
  PDP mistake.
- Images are 50–70% of PDP page weight. AVIF over JPEG can take a 10-image PDP from ~2 MB to
  ~400 KB. Worth wiring into the image pipeline given the catalogue is photo-heavy.
- Mobile is ~70–78% of Sri Lankan e-commerce traffic — optimise mobile and desktop follows.

## 5. Hostinger: the plan Najath is buying

### 5.1 Name and identity

**"Business" was renamed "Unlimited" in July 2026.** Same plan. Existing Business customers keep
their plan; the name still appears on some Website Builder landing pages. So Najath's "business
plan" and the repo's "Unlimited" recommendation are the same thing — no conflict to resolve.

### 5.2 Actual limits (V3, i.e. anything purchased from 2025-12-17 onward)

| | Business/Unlimited |
|---|---|
| Storage | **50 GB NVMe** |
| Websites | 50 |
| Mailboxes | **5** (free 12 months only) |
| Databases | **150** |
| RAM | 3 GB |
| CPU | 2 cores |

Hostinger cut this plan substantially: storage 200→50 GB, websites 100→50, mailboxes 100→5,
databases 300→150. **Inode limits are not published** on the limits page but are reported around
600,000; when hit, no new files *or email* can be received even with free disk. For a
photo-heavy catalogue this is the limit to actually watch — not gigabytes.

Verdict: **still the right plan.** 50 GB and 3 GB RAM are ample for a small catalogue at 5–30
orders/day, and Object Cache — the feature that matters — requires this tier or higher. But the
repo's comparison table oversells it and should be corrected.

### 5.3 What to actually switch on

- **Data centre: Singapore.** Confirmed ~35–60 ms to Colombo across Dialog, SLT-Mobitel and
  Hutch, versus 180–250 ms from Europe and 250–350 ms from the US. Must be chosen at setup.
- **Object Cache** (LiteSpeed Memcached / LSMCD) — Business tier or higher. Enable in hPanel,
  then point LiteSpeed Cache at it.
- **LiteSpeed Cache plugin**, object cache on, lifetime 360s default is fine. Keep QUIC.cloud
  CDN **off** — Cloudflare owns the edge here.
- **WooCommerce session storage** lives in the DB by default (WooCommerce → Settings → Advanced);
  worth reviewing once object cache is live.
- **Staging** — hPanel → WordPress → Staging. Use it for the two DB-updating Woo releases.
- **Git deploy** — hPanel → Advanced → Git, connects via **GitHub OAuth, no SSH key needed**.
  With `najathakram/mavea` now pushed, this is the deployment path for the four custom plugins.
- **SSH + WP-CLI** — hPanel → Advanced → SSH Access.

### 5.4 The email trap

Email is **free for 12 months, then it converts to a paid Hostinger Business Starter
subscription automatically** if a payment method is on file. Two consequences:

1. **Diarise the renewal date at purchase.** It will auto-charge silently.
2. The plan already routes transactional mail through **Brevo via WP Mail SMTP** — keep that.
   Store mail should never depend on hosting mailboxes, and this sidesteps the trap for
   everything except human mail to `hello@mavea.lk`.

Also relevant to the COD flow's synthetic-email subdomain: if nameservers point at Hostinger it
**auto-configures Hostinger Email records**, which would fight the null-MX design. Set that
subdomain's records explicitly after DNS is live.

## 6. Domain: `mavea.lk` → Hostinger, with Cloudflare

### 6.1 The choice that matters

Three-way decision, and the plan already implies the right one:

- **Hostinger nameservers** — simplest, auto-configures Hostinger Email, but gives up Cloudflare.
- **Cloudflare nameservers, A record → Hostinger** ← **this one.** Keeps Cloudflare's Colombo
  PoP in front, which is the entire latency argument in `HOSTING-DECISION.md`.
- Registry DNS pointing at Hostinger IP — no CDN, no reason.

### 6.2 Steps

1. Register `mavea.lk`. **Registrar choice has a consequence:** agents such as provider.lk offer
   **self-service nameserver changes** from a client portal, applied at the registry instantly.
   Registering direct with the LK Domain Registry can mean **filing a ticket with the agent** for
   every nameserver change. Given DNS will be changed more than once during launch, prefer an
   agent with self-service DNS.
2. Create the Cloudflare free account, add `mavea.lk`, note the two Cloudflare nameservers.
3. Set those nameservers at the registrar. Registry-level update is immediate; **global
   propagation 2–24 hours** — .lk is slower than gTLDs, typically about one business day.
4. In hPanel → Websites → "Connect via DNS record" to get the server IP.
5. In Cloudflare DNS: `A @ → <Hostinger IP>` and `A www → <Hostinger IP>`, both **proxied
   (orange)**. Delete any conflicting A/AAAA/CNAME first.
6. Keep **MX and mail records DNS-only (grey)** — proxying mail records breaks email.
7. Cloudflare SSL/TLS → **Full (Strict)**. Hostinger issues valid Let's Encrypt certificates, so
   Strict works. **Never use Flexible** — it causes redirect loops and login/cookie breakage on
   WordPress, and would break wp-admin.
8. SSL/TLS → Edge Certificates → **Always Use HTTPS** on.
9. Cache-bypass rules for `/cart`, `/checkout`, `/my-account`, `/wc-api/*` and WooCommerce
   session cookies — on **both** Cloudflare and LiteSpeed. Getting this wrong is the classic
   "add-to-cart does nothing with warm cache" bug.
10. Verify: root and www both resolve, MX unchanged, SSL A grade, TTFB from Colombo < 500 ms.

`mavea.com` is separate — claim it with Hostinger's free-domain year (gTLD, so it is covered;
`.lk` is a ccTLD and is not) and 301 it to the `.lk`.

## 7. Compliance

**Sri Lanka PDPA No. 9 of 2022.** Sri Lanka was the first South Asian country to pass standalone
data protection law. The Data Protection Authority has been operational since August 2023, **but
as of June 2026 no commencement order for the substantive Parts had been gazetted** — so the
day-to-day obligations are not yet in force.

Do not treat that as permission to skip it: the policy pages are being written anyway for the
PayHere application, and doing it once correctly is cheaper than retrofitting. Publish a real
privacy notice stating what is collected and why, keep the data inventory honest (phone,
address, WhatsApp number, order history), encrypt in transit, restrict admin access, and have a
breach plan. The ops-staff custom role in §6 of the plan is already the right instinct.

**VAT:** not required below LKR 60M/yr — unchanged.
